# ML research notes: thin-data building price-per-sqft ranges

Scope: literature and web evidence for improving a calibrated range for a building's median AED/sqft (one bedroom type) when the building has 1 to 4 recent registered sales.
Method: about 24 web searches (Nov 2026). Most figures come from search-result snippets, abstracts, or secondary summaries, not full texts. Each such figure is marked "(snippet)". Vendor numbers are self-reported.

---

## Summary: what this means for the model choice

- **Keep the partial-pooling core and make it formal.** Multilevel or random-effects hedonic models with nested location levels beat flat models in several studies. Likelihood-ratio tests reject "no random effects", and borrowing across levels can cut uncertainty by more than 50% in a thin sub-sample (Philadelphia Fed land study). Our inverse-variance blend is already an informal empirical-Bayes shrinker. A fitted mixed model (district > community > project > building, with bedroom effects, log size and a monthly index) is the natural next step. It estimates the shrinkage weights from data instead of hand-setting them.
- **Boosting wins on large, data-rich benchmarks, not on thin groups.** GBM was the most accurate of six methods on 123k Swiss sales, and GBRT beat hedonic regression in Singapore, Netherlands and US land studies. None of these tested buildings with 1 to 4 sales. A good use for boosting here is a *residual* model on top of the mixed model (the GPBoost / MERF / mixed-effect-GBM idea). Any building encoding must be out-of-fold or ordered (CatBoost-style), or it leaks.
- **Time-adjust older comparables.** Appraisal research expects 2.5 to 9% drift over about 6 months (FHFA). Hedonic indices are more robust than repeat-sales in small samples. A monthly hedonic or hierarchical index (community or district level, smoothed) should be applied before pooling older sales.
- **Size needs an explicit elasticity, not a flat AED/sqft.** Elasticities of price with respect to area are about 1.03 to 1.09 in Paris (that is, roughly flat AED/sqft), but they vary by segment and are non-linear in Taipei, Hong Kong and Dubai (Worku 2017). Estimate a log-size slope per bedroom type or segment and adjust comparables to the target size. Appraisers' marginal $/sqft is well below average $/sqft.
- **Intervals: keep split conformal, add normalization and groups.** CQR gives valid marginal coverage and adaptive widths, and it was shown on house prices in Bastos and Paquette (2025). Exact conditional coverage is impossible (Barber et al. 2019/2021). Mondrian or group-conditional calibration by evidence class is the right compromise. Clustering groups with similar score distributions reduced per-group coverage gaps in a Norwegian study.
- **Guard against time shift.** Calibration data are 36 months of a moving market. Weighted conformal (covariate shift) or adaptive conformal inference (online alpha update) keeps coverage under drift. At minimum, calibrate on the most recent window and monitor rolling coverage.
- **Benchmark targets.** Industry reports MdAPE, PE10 and PE20, and FSD. A rule of thumb is PE10 of at least 50% (Kirchmeyer 2004), and strong national AVMs claim PE10 above 80%. Thin and rural markets are much worse (indicative median error 10 to 15%, snippet). Report MdAPE, PE10 and PE20 per evidence class, plus the coverage and width of the ranges.
- **Dubai specifics.** DLD's own House Price Index is a log-linear hedonic model on registered transactions, with outlier cut-offs of AED 250k and AED 6.4M (snippet). Off-plan carries a large AED/sqft premium in transaction data (Reidin: an average community gap of 33.6%, 45.4% post-COVID, and 53% citywide in April 2024). So ready versus off-plan must be a model term, not something to pool across. Valuation reports fall under the DLD's Emirates Book of Valuation Standards (IVS-aligned) and registered valuers. A range of registered sales is not a valuation, and the site should say so.

## 1. AVMs in thin markets and accuracy metrics

- **Definitions.** PE10 or PE20 is the share of estimates within ±10% or ±20% of the sale price. MdAPE is the median absolute percentage error. Hit rate is the share of properties the AVM can value at all. FSD (forecast standard deviation) is the model's own error dispersion and is the usual basis for confidence scores. Accuracy must be read together with hit rate: "90% PE10 on 50% of properties" is not the same as "70% PE10 on 95%". Sources: "AVM Accuracy Metrics Explained" (vendor explainer, 2026) https://valuations.andrewgadsden.com/knowledge/accuracy-metrics ; Clear Capital, "AVM Testing: A Short Glossary" https://www.clearcapital.com/blog-avm-testing-glossary/
- **What counts as good.** Kirchmeyer (2004) suggested at least 50% of AVM values should fall within ±10% of the sale price. A vendor page calls PE10 above 80% strong for a national AVM (snippet). One vendor reported PE20 of 84.5% on one page and 82.3% on another for the same 147,239-sale test, so treat vendor numbers with caution (snippet). Source: same andrewgadsden.com pages.
- **FSD to interval.** Under normal errors, a band of 1.28 × FSD covers about 80% and a band of 1.645 × FSD covers about 90%. Example: FSD 13% gives about ±16.6% at 80%. Confidence score is often defined as 100 − FSD, but this is not standardized across vendors. Freddie Mac says lenders should validate FSDs out of sample. Sources: Freddie Mac, "Metrics Matter" white paper https://my.sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheets/dougwhitepaper_metricsmatter.pdf ; "Forecast Standard Deviation (FSD) Explained" https://valuations.andrewgadsden.com/knowledge/forecast-standard-deviation
- **Thin markets are harder.** In rural US markets, comparables are fewer and homes more heterogeneous. More than 25% of rural appraisals came in over 5% above contract price, against 12.7% urban. The authors then test ML and tree-based AVMs for rural areas (snippet). Source: Bogin and Shui, "Appraisal Accuracy and Automated Valuation Models in Rural Areas", FHFA WP 18-03 (2018), JREFE 60:40–52 (2020) https://www.fhfa.gov/document/wp1803.pdf
- **Small markets, ML.** Gnat (2021, *Land*) found KNN and XGBoost more resistant to a low number of observations on small, underdeveloped markets (snippet). Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jlands/v10y2021i4p388-d532550.html
- **Government test (Wales, 2026).** Models did better in rural and semi-rural areas and much worse in heterogeneous urban Cardiff. A nearest-neighbour comparables model did not meet operational accuracy thresholds (snippet). Source: "Testing land valuation methods", Tesseract Academy report for Welsh Government (2026) https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2026-03/testing-land-valuation-methods-tesseract-academy-report.pdf
- **Indicative thin-market error.** Commercial blogs cite median errors of 10 to 15% in thinly traded areas. This is not peer-reviewed. Source: lowcode.agency blog (snippet).
- **Regulation (US).** Interagency AVM quality-control standards (2023/2024) require confidence, data integrity, protection against manipulation, random sample testing and non-discrimination. Source: FDIC, "Quality Control Standards for Automated Valuation Models" (2023) https://www.fdic.gov/regulations/laws/Federal/2023/2023-quality-control-standards-for-automated-valuation-models-3064-ae68-c-008.pdf

**Implication for us:** report MdAPE, PE10, PE20 and "hit rate" (share of buildings with a published range) per evidence class. Treat FSD-style widths as claims to back-test, not facts. Expect 1-to-4-sale buildings to sit near thin-market error levels (MdAPE around 8 to 15%), not near national-AVM levels.

## 2. Hierarchical / mixed-effects hedonic models and empirical Bayes

- **Three-level random effects.** One study fits transactions in census tracts in neighbourhoods. A likelihood-ratio test rejects the no-random-effects model, and the floor-area price is allowed to vary by neighbourhood (random slopes). Source: Economics Bulletin 32(2) (2012) https://www.accessecon.com/Pubs/EB/2012/Volume32/EB-12-V32-I2-P141.pdf
- **Partial pooling by suburb.** A gamma hierarchical model with suburb random intercepts fit best and captured variation between suburbs. Source: Zewotir and North, *Journal of Property Research* 38(3):198–212 (2021) https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jpropr/v38y2021i3p198-212.html
- **Multilevel STAR (Bayesian, 4 levels).** Coefficients at each level can follow their own regressions, which allows non-linear and smooth spatial effects. Source: Brunauer, Lang, Umlauf, *Statistical Modelling* 13(2):95–123 (2013) https://statmod.org/smij/Vol13/Iss2/Brunauer/Abstract.html
- **Borrowing strength halves uncertainty.** A multilevel Bayesian model pools many improved-property sales with few vacant-land sales. The extra information cut estimation uncertainty by more than 50%, and shrinkage damped outlier influence on individual parcels (snippet). Source: Albouy and Shin, Philadelphia Fed WP 22-38 (2022) https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/working-papers/2022/wp22-38.pdf
- **Few transactions.** Plain regression is unsatisfactory with about 10 to 30 purchases, and outlier detection gets hard. The authors propose robust Bayesian estimation with priors. Source: Dorndorf et al., FIG Working Week (2016) https://fig.net/resources/proceedings/fig_proceedings/fig2016/papers/ts05h/TS05H_dorndorf_soot_et_al_8187.pdf
- **Shrinkage in AVM specification.** A Berlin model with about 3,800 variables gave extreme errors under OLS, which made shrinkage necessary. Source: Hinrichs, Kolbe, Werwatz, *JPR* 38(2):130–153 (2021) https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jpropr/v38y2021i2p130-153.html
- **Caveats.** Over-shrinkage is common in small-area estimation. Shrinking variances, not only means, matters for noisy small groups. One HLM study found almost all residual variance at the apartment level, not the neighbourhood level. Sources: "Bayesian Estimators for Small Area Models Shrinking Both Means and Variances" https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.05179 ; "Bayesian Logistic Regression for Small Areas" https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.00446 ; Morris, "Parametric Empirical Bayes Inference" JASA (1983) https://errorstatistics.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/morris.pdf
- **Mixed effects vs ML (Swiss data).** Mixed-effects regression was one of six methods tested. It was beaten by gradient boosting on overall accuracy (see section 3).

**Implication for us:** fit a nested random-intercept model (district/community/project/building) on log AED/sqft, with bedroom × ready/off-plan fixed effects, log size, and a month index. Consider random slopes for bedroom at the project level. Use the building BLUP and its posterior variance as the point and spread. Let the data set the shrinkage, but check for over-shrinkage of distinctive buildings, which tend to be premium towers.

## 3. Gradient boosting, target encoding and residual boosting

- **GBM best of six (Swiss).** On 123k single-family sales in Switzerland (2005–2017), gradient boosting was most accurate, ahead of OLS, robust regression, mixed effects, random forest and neural nets. Robust regression gave the least volatile predictions. Moving versus extending windows mattered little. Source: Mayer, Bourassa, Hoesli, Scognamiglio, "Estimation and updating methods for hedonic valuation", *JERER* 12(1):134–150 (2019) https://www.doi.org/10.1108/JERER-08-2018-0035
- **Singapore AVM.** Boosted trees beat a single decision tree and multiple regression. Out-of-sample errors on 2018 sales were about 5 to 9% (snippet). Source: Sing, Yang, Yu, JREFE (2021) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8568682
- **Other benchmarks.** GBRT cut out-of-sample errors substantially on 2 of 3 datasets against stepwise regression (Erasmus 2005, https://repub.eur.nl/pub/7145/ei2005-50.pdf). In a US land study, gradient-boosted trees with clustering beat hedonic regression and a stacked ensemble did best (BEA WP2023-4, https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/papers/BEA-WP2023-4.pdf). For a review of automated appraisal methods, see "From human business to machine learning" (2022) https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9294847/
- **Target-encoding leakage.** A naive target statistic uses the row's own label. For a category seen once, the encoding *is* the label. CatBoost's ordered target statistics use only earlier rows in a random permutation, and they beat greedy, holdout and leave-one-out TS in the paper's experiments. Rare categories are still noisy, so smoothing toward a prior is needed. Source: Prokhorenkova et al., "CatBoost: unbiased boosting with categorical features", NeurIPS 2018 https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.09516
- **Boosting plus random effects.** GPBoost combines tree boosting with grouped (nested or crossed) random effects and Gaussian processes. It generalizes both mixed models and plain boosting. Sigrist, arXiv 2004.02653 (2020); JMLR (2022) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.02653 ; https://github.com/fabsig/GPBoost . Mixed-effect gradient boosting has been used for small-area estimation, arXiv 2406.04256 (2024) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04256 . GBMixed jointly boosts mean and variance components, arXiv 2511.00217 (2025) https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00217v1 . No housing-specific GPBoost benchmark was found.
- **Interpretability.** Constrained GBTs with SHAP isolated location value in about 300k Swiss rents and kept high accuracy. Source: Stalder, Mayer, Bourassa, Hoesli, SFI WP 25-02 (2025) https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-25-02-isolating-location-value-using-shap-and-interaction-constraints
- **Caveat.** CatBoost vs LightGBM comparisons (Ames, King County) favour CatBoost slightly but are single, non-thin datasets. Source: arXiv 2409.02130 (2024) https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02130v2

**Implication for us:** use boosting on residuals of the mixed model, with features such as size, month, ready/off-plan, parking and bedroom, plus *out-of-fold* building and project encodings. Do not use raw building target encodings: with 1 to 4 sales per building, in-fold encodings leak almost the whole label. Evaluate with leave-building-out or time-forward splits.

## 4. Time indices and time-adjusting comparables

- **Hedonic indices are more robust in small samples.** A Dutch comparison found hedonic indices less sensitive to small segments than mix-adjusted indices. Schwann (1998) found the hedonic index fairly robust in small samples, though a time-series variant did better in thin markets. Repeat-sales uses only a minority of sales and needs enough repeat pairs. Sources: RBA RDP 2006-03 "Alternative Measures of House Prices" https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/2006/2006-03/alternative-measures-of-house-prices.html ; Schwann, JREFE 16(3):269–287 (1998) https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jrefec/v16y1998i3p269-87.html
- **Repeat-sales coverage.** Repeat-sales indices use about one fifth of sales on average and get noisy when trading slows (practitioner, snippet). Source: Collateral Analytics https://www.collateralanalytics.com/what-do-we-really-want-from-residential-real-estate-available-and-proposed-indices/
- **Hierarchical repeat sales.** A common trend plus cluster-specific deviations with t-distributed errors (Kalman filter, Bayesian) gives reliable granular indices from few observations. Source: Francke and van de Minne, JREFE 55(4):511–532 (2017) https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/111842 . A 2025 extension uses a spatial dynamic factor model for census-tract indices from sparse data: https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/270378264/s11146-023-09957-w.pdf
- **Japan.** The transaction market is far thinner than goods markets, so monthly indices are hard. Hedonic methods are standard for official statistics. Source: Hitotsubashi WP https://hermes-ir.lib.hit-u.ac.jp/hermes/ir/re/27717/wp034.pdf
- **Size of time adjustments.** For comparables about 6 months old, expected time adjustments are about 2.5 to 9% of price on average. Appraisers adjusted fewer than 10% of comps over much of the period. The Appraisal Institute disputes the method because it relies on Zillow AVM data. Sources: FHFA WP 24-07 (2024) https://fhfa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/wp2407.pdf ; FHFA blog https://www.fhfa.gov/blog/insights/underutilization-of-appraisal-time-adjustments ; Appraisal Institute (2024) https://www.appraisalinstitute.org/insights-and-resources/insights/newsroom/AI-news-releases/2024-12-13-time-adjustment-research-contains-multiple-flaws
- **Rule.** Fannie Mae requires a check of market-condition change between the comp contract date and the effective date. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-1.3-09 https://guide-selling.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-1.3-09/adjustments-comparable-sales

**Implication for us:** with Dubai's fast-moving market over 36 months, older building sales need time adjustment. Use a monthly hedonic index (month fixed effects or a smoothed random walk) at district or community level, shrunk toward the city level, and estimated jointly in the mixed model. Repeat-sales is a poor fit, because pairs are rare and off-plan to ready flips mix product states.

## 5. Comparable-sales approaches (k-NN, learned similarity)

- **Learned comp selection.** A 2026 paper argues that comp selection usually uses fixed temporal or geographic heuristics. It learns the selection with retrieval-enhanced ML and reports better comparables than traditional selection (snippet). Source: arXiv 2603.12986 (2026) https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12986v1
- **Valuation-anchored metric learning.** A CatBoost-learned similarity used in weighted k-NN gave lower MAE and RMSE than Euclidean, Gower or embedding distances at every k tested (private-markets data, not housing). Source: "What Makes a Peer? Valuation-Anchored Similarity in Private Markets", arXiv 2608.12594 (2026) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12594
- **Thin-data robustness.** KNN was among the methods most resistant to few observations (Gnat 2021, section 1). But a Welsh government nearest-neighbour model failed operational thresholds in heterogeneous urban areas (section 1).
- **Practice.** Comps are adjusted by paired sales. Size adjustments use marginal, not average, $/sqft (section 8). Time adjustments are often skipped (section 4). Practitioner guides suggest k = 5 to 10 similarity-weighted comps (blog, not authoritative). Source: https://theneuralbase.com/ai-for-real-estate/learn/intermediate/comparables-adjustments/

**Implication for us:** our "reference median from sibling buildings" is a hierarchical k-NN. A learned similarity, for example weights on project, community, district, size band, ready status and price tier fitted to minimise leave-building-out error, could replace fixed levels. It must still feed adjusted (time + size) comps, and its gain over the mixed model's random effects should be shown out of sample before adopting it.

## 6. Quantile regression, CQR and normalized split conformal

- **CQR.** CQR combines quantile regression with split conformal. It gives finite-sample, distribution-free marginal coverage for any QR estimator, adapts to heteroscedasticity, and gave shorter intervals than other conformal methods on 11 datasets. Source: Romano, Patterson, Candès, "Conformalized Quantile Regression", NeurIPS 2019, arXiv 1905.03222 https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03222 (code: github.com/yromano/cqr)
- **House-price evidence.** On San Francisco Bay Area sales, CQR intervals hit nominal coverage, while plain QR intervals under-covered. Intervals adapted to dwelling characteristics, and larger and older homes were harder (snippet). Source: Bastos and Paquette, "On the uncertainty of real estate price predictions", *JPR* 42(1):1–19 (2025) https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jpropr/v42y2025i1p1-19.html ; WP https://rem.rc.iseg.ulisboa.pt/wps/pdf/REM_WP_0314_2024.pdf
- **Normalized scores.** The score is R = |y − ŷ| / σ̂(x), where σ̂ is a difficulty estimate (a residual model, ensemble spread, or kNN distance). Lei et al. (2018) show it keeps validity, and intervals widen where the model is less reliable. Source: "Conformal Prediction: a Unified Review", arXiv 2005.07972 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07972
- **With LightGBM.** Locally weighted Pearson residuals kept nominal coverage with the smallest average width in simulations. Source: arXiv 2507.06921 (2025) https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06921v1
- **Alternatives.** Score reweighting with quantile regression forests (arXiv 2303.12695, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.12695) and regression trees for adaptive intervals (arXiv 2402.07357). No result shows which σ̂ estimator is best in general.

**Implication for us:** our "multiplier by evidence class" is a coarse normalized conformal. Better: score = |log y − log ŷ| / σ̂, with σ̂ from the mixed model's posterior SD (or a residual model). Then take the conformal quantile within each class. CQR is an option if a boosted quantile model is fitted, but with very small per-class calibration sets, a normalized score on a good σ̂ is simpler and more stable.

## 7. Mondrian / group-conditional coverage and time shift

- **Mondrian CP.** Partitioning the calibration set into disjoint groups gives 1 − α coverage within each group (Vovk et al. 2003). Class-conditional CP is the special case where the groups are labels. Kandinsky CP extends this to overlapping groups. Source: "pintervals" R package paper, arXiv 2601.03994 (2026) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03994 ; summary https://api.emergentmind.com/topics/conformal-prediction-with-conditional-guarantees
- **Impossibility.** No finite-length interval can give distribution-free coverage conditional on an exact x. Approximate conditional coverage can be reached trivially from marginal coverage at a stricter level. Source: Barber, Candès, Ramdas, Tibshirani, "The limits of distribution-free conditional predictive inference", *Information and Inference* (2021), arXiv 1903.04684 https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04684
- **Clustered conformal on housing.** For Norwegian house prices, 286 municipalities were clustered into classes with similar score distributions. This lowered the mean absolute per-municipality coverage gap (snippet). Source: Hjort, COPA 2024 "Clustered conformal prediction" https://www.mn.uio.no/math/english/people/aca/anderdh/copa_2024__clustered_conformal_prediction.pdf
- **Selection bias.** If intervals are shown only for selected units, for example high-priced ones, ordinary CP loses its guarantee for those units. Selection-aware methods fix this. Source: arXiv 2301.00584 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.00584
- **Covariate shift.** Weight each calibration score by the test/train likelihood ratio, then take a weighted quantile. Coverage holds under covariate shift when the ratio is known or estimated. Source: Tibshirani, Barber, Candès, Ramdas, "Conformal Prediction Under Covariate Shift", NeurIPS 2019 https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019
- **Adaptive conformal inference.** Update α_{t+1} = α_t + γ(α − err_t) online. This gives long-run coverage with no assumptions on the data-generating process and was robust to visible shifts on real data. Source: Gibbs and Candès, NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2106.00170 https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170

**Implication for us:** keep Mondrian calibration by evidence class, but merge classes with similar score distributions so each has enough calibration points. Check per-class coverage on a time-forward hold-out. Because the market drifts, weight recent calibration points more heavily (a simple form of weighted CP) or run ACI on the monthly realized coverage. Do not promise per-building coverage, which is impossible. State that coverage is per evidence class.

## 8. Size and price per square foot

- **Paris.** The elasticity of price with respect to apartment size is about 1.09 for the cheapest units and 1.03 for the most expensive. A related paper gives 1.07 for the cheapest decile. Elasticities above 1 mean price per m² rises slightly with size in that market, against the usual premise (snippet). Source: THEMA WP 2016-11 (Cergy) https://thema.u-cergy.fr/IMG/pdf/2016-11.pdf
- **Taipei.** Total price rises at a decreasing rate for small units (diminishing marginal price), and the pattern differs by price tier. Source: *IJSPM* (Apr 2021), "Non-linear relationships between house size and price" https://doaj.org/article/a5ae914ab8434aec8fcfeef95dec738d
- **Hong Kong.** The rent-size elasticity for very small subdivided units is about half that of whole flats at a quarter of average size, which suggests convexity in size elasticity. Source: Leung et al., "Size elasticity on housing rent", PRRES https://www.prres.org/uploads/564/2352/Leung_Size_elasticity_on_housing_rent.pdf
- **Specification.** Semi-log models in floor area plus room count can be misspecified, because the marginal price of space should depend on space. Source: Diewert, Huang, Burnett-Isaacs (FGV paper) https://eventos.fgv.br/sites/eventos.fgv.br/files/arquivos/u161/erwin_diewert_ning_huang_kate_burnett-isaacs_-_paper_0.pdf
- **Dubai.** Size sensitivity varies across the price distribution in a double-log quantile hedonic model on 140,039 transactions. The coefficient was not visible in the abstract. Source: Worku, "House price drivers in Dubai: nonlinearity and heterogeneity", *IJHMA* 10(3):384–409 (2017) https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/ijhmap/ijhma-06-2016-0048.html
- **Appraiser practice.** Whole-property $/sqft bundles location, view and other features, so applying it to a size gap overstates the size effect. Marginal GLA adjustments are smaller, for example $25 to $75/sqft where totals exceed $200/sqft. They come from paired sales and shrink for larger homes (forum and blog sources, not authoritative). Sources: https://appraisersblogs.com/gross-living-area-gla-adjustment ; https://appraisersforum.com/forums/threads/condition-square-footage-adjustments.227372

**Implication for us:** within a bedroom type, model log AED/sqft = … + (β − 1)·log(size), and estimate β per bedroom type (and possibly per segment) from the data. The literature gives no universal sign: β near 1.03 to 1.09 in Paris, below 1 for small units elsewhere. Size-adjust each comparable to the target bedroom's typical size before taking medians. Show the elasticity estimate in diagnostics.

## 9. Dubai / UAE specifics

- **DLD House Price Index.** The index uses a log-linear hedonic model on DLD registered transactions. Coefficients are the marginal contributions of attributes, and logs are used to reduce heteroscedasticity. Apartment and villa models use different variables, and outliers below AED 250k and above AED 6.4M are excluded (search summary; we could not extract the PDF text). Source: DLD, "Dubai House Price Index: Methodology" https://dubailand.gov.ae/media/qnslugbi/the-dubai-house-price-index-methodology.pdf . There is a commercial counterpart: "Dubai CPPI Methodology" (2021) https://dubailand.gov.ae/media/wmhp431w/fin_dubai-cppi-methodology-2021.pdf
- **Academic hedonic study.** Worku (2017): 140,039 transactions over 10 years, double-log quantile regression. Size, location, type and regulation effects are non-linear and heterogeneous across submarkets. Few attributes were available. See section 8.
- **Off-plan vs ready.** In transaction data, off-plan apartments sold at higher average AED/sqft than ready apartments citywide since January 2012. The average community-level gap was 33.6%, 45.4% post-COVID, and the citywide premium was 53% at end-April 2024. Source: Reidin, "Dubai Residential: The New Build Effect" (2024) https://reidin.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Dubai-Residential-The-New-Build-Effect-Examining-the-Difference-in-Price-Action-for-Ready-and-Off-Plan-Apartments.pdf . Brokerage blogs claim the opposite (a 15 to 30% off-plan discount, or that "the gap has nearly vanished" in 2025) without data, for example https://famproperties.com/blog/dubai-property-off-plan-vs-ready-2025 . Older Gulf News/Reidin figures were mixed by area (Business Bay: off-plan AED 1,625 vs ready AED 1,414 psf; Downtown: AED 1,922 vs AED 1,979).
- **Price levels (practitioner).** Apartments averaged about AED 2,006/sqft citywide (Feb 2026), about AED 1,448 in JVC and about AED 2,980 in Downtown. Source: Engel & Völkers https://www.engelvoelkers.com/ae/en/resources/average-price-per-square-foot-in-dubai
- **Smart Rental Index 2025.** DLD launched it on 2 Jan 2025. Each building gets a 1 to 5 star rating from more than 60 criteria (structure, finishes, maintenance, location, services), alongside area and building average rents and old and new contracts. Allowed increases run from 0% (rent less than 10% below average) up to 20% (more than 40% below average, per Decree 43/2013 bands). Residential only for now. Sources: DLD announcement https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/news-media/smart-rental-index-announcement ; WAM https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bhhk3ia-dubai-land-department-launches-smart-rental-index ; Khaleej Times https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/new-dubai-smart-rental-index-landlords-must-improve-property-ratings-to-hike-rents . (Note: one summary described the top band as "rents more than 40% above average"; the official bands concern how far rent sits *below* the index average. Check the DLD page.)

**Implication for us:** ready versus off-plan must be a fixed effect, possibly interacted with community, and never pooled blindly. The gap is large and time-varying. DLD's own index uses a log-linear hedonic model, which supports a log AED/sqft mixed model as the defensible core. Consider similar outlier cut-offs. The site should label outputs as "range of registered sales / statistical estimate, not a valuation under EBVS/RICS". The rental index's building star rating is a possible future building-quality covariate if it becomes accessible.
